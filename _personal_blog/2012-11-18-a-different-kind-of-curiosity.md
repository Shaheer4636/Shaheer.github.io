By my teenage years, curiosity had changed shape. It was no longer just about finding out what a computer could do. It was about understanding why systems behaved the way they did, where they broke, and what made one design feel elegant while another felt fragile.

That shift mattered. It brought me from casual interest into more deliberate study. I started paying attention to patterns, tradeoffs, and the difference between a solution that looked impressive and one that actually held up in practice.

That has stayed with me in my professional life. The most useful work usually comes from asking the right questions, not from trying to impress people with complexity.
