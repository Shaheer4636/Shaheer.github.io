I was still young when I first started paying attention to how computers worked. What struck me then was not the hardware itself, but the sense that a screen could hold possibilities. A few clicks could open a new world. That feeling stayed with me long after the first excitement faded.

I did not understand much at the time. That was part of the appeal. I was simply curious. I wanted to know what made things happen, why certain actions produced certain results, and how a machine could respond to a person in a way that felt almost deliberate.

That early curiosity became the seed for the way I work today. A lot of good engineering begins with the same impulse. You notice something, you ask a question, and you keep going until the answer feels real.
